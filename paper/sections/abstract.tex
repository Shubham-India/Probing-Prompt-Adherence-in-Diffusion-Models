\begin{abstract}
Text-to-image diffusion models often violate part of a compositional prompt. We ask when, along the denoising trajectory, each kind of constraint (object presence, count, colour binding, spatial relation, relative size) stops depending on the remaining randomness, and what classifier-free guidance (CFG) changes about that timing. We measure it interventionally: saved trajectory states of Stable Diffusion~1.5 are re-noised and re-denoised, and the agreement of outcomes, corrected for chance agreement between independent seeds, gives a persistence curve $C(t)$ and its half-point $t_{50}$. Hypotheses, decision rules and development-set constants were frozen in a signed protocol before any test image existed. A human audit validated the automatic judge only for object presence.
Six of nine test shards were generated, so all test results are partial and exploratory. The one registered rule that passed concerns guidance: stronger CFG makes object presence persistent earlier ($t_{50}(w{=}15)-t_{50}(w{=}2)=\rbHdReg$ of 40 steps, 95\% CI $[\rbHdRegLo,\rbHdRegHi]$, Holm $p=\rbHdHolm$). The ordering is not an artefact of the chance correction, but an independent-judge-error stress test at the audited error rate removes it. Cross-attention shows the same direction: on identical initial noise, higher CFG separates object nouns and their attention stabilises earlier, and objects that are later missed already show diffuse, overlapping attention in the first steps. A guidance schedule timed by the development-set measurement (Commitment-Aligned Guidance) failed its registered test. Its switch steps did not transfer to the test scenes, and matched-mean controls attribute its diversity gain to lower average guidance rather than to timing. Commitment times also did not replicate across splits, and a full-resample control revealed a presence offset that we characterise but cannot yet explain.
\end{abstract}
