\section{Discussion}
What the evidence directly establishes today is limited to (i) the pilot's exploratory descriptive results with wide intervals, (ii) the integrity and reproducibility of the first Kaggle runs for the scope tested, and (iii) design facts such as the share of images in which all required objects are detected under the first-pass judge. It suggests, but cannot show, that missing objects dominate failures and that early attention carries some information beyond design variables. It leaves open whether constraint outcomes differ in persistence timing, whether attention adds information beyond noise and prompt structure, and whether edits are time-gated.
A bounded-null or inconclusive HA, HB or HC result would be reported as such, and the fallback contributions (benchmark, evaluator audit, controlled CFG study) remain valid. Alternative explanations for any persistence difference include judge noise, the dependence of the re-noising magnitude on the grid step, selection through presence conditioning, and prompt-specific object priors.
