\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What CFG changes about timing, and how sure we are.} Across three guidance scales, object presence becomes persistent earlier under stronger guidance. Under a restart that re-noises by eight steps, a trajectory at $w=15$ has settled whether its objects appear several steps before one at $w=2$. Three observations make this more than a statistical accident of one estimator:
\begin{itemize}
\item the ordering holds in uncorrected agreement;
\item it survives subtracting the unexplained offset of the full-resample control;
\item cross-attention shows a parallel shift on identical initial noise: object nouns separate earlier and their maps stabilise about ten steps earlier.
\end{itemize}
Three things limit it.
\begin{itemize}
\item Only three guidance levels were measured, so we cannot describe the shape of the relation.
\item The contrast rests on the 67 scenes with branches at both extremes and reverses in the counting scenes.
\item Correcting for a judge that errs independently on 14\% of images, the audited rate, removes it.
\end{itemize}
Our audit measured how often the presence judge errs, not whether its errors repeat across the seeds of a scene, and that is the property the conclusion depends on. A seed-paired audit, labelling several seeds of the same prompt, is the cheapest way to settle it.

\paragraph{Attention shows where things will be, not what has been bound.} The saved attention maps add two things the restart probe cannot. First, objects that the judge later misses differ from found objects of the same scene from the first denoising steps: their noun attention is less localised and overlaps more with the other nouns. Object neglect is therefore visible long before the image is decided, consistent with early-attention forecasting \citep{diffusionprobe2026,guo2024initno} and with the premise of attention-based corrections \citep{chefer2023attend}. Second, the registered attention-stabilisation statistic fails as a stand-in for persistence across constraint types, for a specific reason. Its binding signal is the colour word's map, which follows the object's location and so stabilises with the layout, around step 8 at $w=7.5$. Restart persistence of the binding itself comes about twelve steps later. Attention stabilisation is a reasonable proxy for when the layout settles, and a poor one for when an attribute is bound.

\paragraph{Commitment times are not yet a stable property.} The development split suggested that colour assignment is fixed at least as early as object presence and that numerosity stays open longest. The test scenes do not reproduce this: binding becomes persistent after presence under both restart probes. Several causes could produce the shift and the partial data cannot separate them:
\begin{itemize}
\item a different scene composition;
\item the DEV precision gate that had already failed;
\item the unvalidated binding judge;
\item left-censoring of the DEV binding curve.
\end{itemize}
We draw one methodological conclusion: a commitment time estimated on about 100 scenes, with intervals wider than $\pm7$ steps, is too imprecise to schedule an intervention on.

\paragraph{Why CAG did not help.} CAG was meant to spend guidance while a constraint is still open and release it afterwards. It failed for two identifiable reasons. Its switch steps came from DEV commitment times that did not transfer, so on the test scenes it lowers guidance at step 12 while binding is still unsettled. And its registered comparison confounds timing with dose: every CAG arm applies less mean guidance than constant $w=7.5$, and the matched-mean controls attribute the diversity gain to that lower mean. At equal mean guidance, front-loading is the least diverse of the three orders and no better in adherence. This agrees with interval guidance \citep{kynkaanniemi2024interval}, where guidance at the highest noise levels harms diversity, and with step selection \citep{grimal2025step}, where late conditioning helps alignment more than early conditioning. The matched-mean design is what made this diagnosis possible, and we recommend it for any guidance-schedule claim. Online, prompt-dependent schedules \citep{dynamiccfg2025} avoid the transfer problem by measuring during sampling and are the natural comparison for a revised CAG.

\paragraph{The estimator's open validity question.} The full-resample control was meant to show $C\approx0$ for independent draws. Its registered gate could not pass at this sample size, which we only recognised after the fact. Its presence offset is real, though, and unexplained: either the fixed four-seed noise panel makes $A_0$ unrepresentative, or base and branch images differ in a way the identity checks did not cover. Both explanations bias absolute $t_{50}$ more than contrasts between conditions. A 400-image experiment would separate them (Section~\ref{sec:resDEV}). Until then, absolute commitment steps in this paper should be read as probe- and panel-relative.
