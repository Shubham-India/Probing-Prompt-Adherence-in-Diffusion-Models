\section{Conclusion}
We asked when prompt constraints become persistent during diffusion sampling, what classifier-free guidance changes about that timing, whether cross-attention reveals it, and whether a guidance schedule timed by the measurement is useful. We answered with an interventional, chance-corrected estimator under a signed pre-registration, and reported every gate as it fell.

On the six available test shards, all exploratory: stronger guidance makes object presence persistent earlier, the only registered rule that passed, but the result holds only if the presence judge's errors are not independent across seeds. Commitment times measured on the development split did not transfer to the test scenes; attention stabilisation did not track persistence; and Commitment-Aligned Guidance, scheduled from those development times, was neither non-inferior to constant guidance nor better than its reverse. Its diversity gain is a dose effect, which matched-mean controls exposed.

The broader lesson concerns precision. Timing differences of a few steps are only interpretable with a judge that is substantially more accurate than current detector-based judges for anything beyond presence, and with commitment estimates far tighter than about 100 development scenes provide. Two things remain: the registered confirmatory analysis, which needs the three missing test shards, and a seed-paired judge audit, which would decide whether the guidance-timing effect is real.
