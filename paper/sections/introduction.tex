\section{Introduction}
Prompt adherence is usually reported as one score per image. Compositional failures are constraint-specific, and the constraints may be resolved at different moments of the sampling trajectory. If they are, guidance that matters early may be wasted late, diagnostics that read early signals may be forecasts for some constraints and read-outs for others, and a pooled score can hide all of this.

This is a mechanistic and evaluation study with one simple intervention; it does not propose a new generative model. The bounded contributions, each conditional on the pre-specified tests, are: (i) a chance-corrected, two-probe measurement of restart persistence for presence, count, relative size, spatial relation and colour binding; (ii) a test of whether CFG acts by moving these steps (HD), with the chance level computed separately at each guidance scale so that reduced seed diversity is not mistaken for earlier commitment; (iii) a test of whether cross-attention stabilisation tracks the causal measurement (HF); (iv) Commitment-Aligned Guidance (CAG), a training-free schedule whose switch step is read off development-set measurements, compared with constant, reversed, matched-average and global-switch schedules (HE); and (v) a scene-graph benchmark with counterfactual and meaning-preserving variants, a relative-size family and presence-conditional outcomes, with a blinded human audit of the evaluator.
Prior work shows that denoising stages control different properties, that guidance can be scheduled, and that timing matters for attention-based corrections; we do not claim the general idea is new, and novelty beyond Section~\ref{sec:related} is stated only as ``to our knowledge'' until the full-text review is complete. We use the operational term restart persistence; we do not claim the model ``decides''.
